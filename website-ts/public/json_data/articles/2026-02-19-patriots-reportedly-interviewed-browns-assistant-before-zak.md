The New England Patriots’ search for a new defensive coordinator extended beyond their own building, with Yahoo Sports reporting the team interviewed Browns assistant Ephraim Banda before ultimately promoting inside linebackers coach Zak Kuhr. The move underscores how seriously New England weighed outside options as it reshapes its staff, and it offers an early glimpse at how the Patriots are balancing continuity with fresh ideas following a coaching transition.

Yahoo Sports reported Banda was “among the coaching candidates interviewed” for the defensive coordinator role. Banda’s connection to Cleveland is notable given the Browns’ recent defensive identity and the leaguewide attention paid to successful defensive staffs. For New England, bringing in Banda for an interview signaled an open-minded process rather than a foregone conclusion that the job would stay in-house.

The Patriots instead elevated Kuhr, turning to a coach already familiar with their personnel and day-to-day operation. While teams often choose continuity when appointing a coordinator — particularly on defense, where communication and terminology can be central to execution — the fact that New England still interviewed candidates like Banda suggests the organization took a broader look at its schematic direction and teaching approach before making a final call.

Banda’s inclusion on the interview list also reflects the NFL’s annual coaching carousel dynamics: assistants from well-regarded units frequently draw coordinator interest, even if it does not immediately lead to a promotion. Interviews, even those that do not result in hires, can establish relationships that matter in future cycles and provide teams with a clearer sense of how candidates would fit their roster and organizational structure.
