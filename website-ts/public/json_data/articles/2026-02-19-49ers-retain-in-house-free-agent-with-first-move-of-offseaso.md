The San Francisco 49ers opened their offseason business by opting for continuity on the offensive line, retaining in-house free agent Nick Zakelj after a season in which he played a limited role. The move, first reported by Yahoo Sports NFL, gives the 49ers a familiar depth option as they begin shaping a roster that expects to contend again and must balance keeping the core intact with managing the realities of the salary cap.

Zakelj’s return is notable less for headline value than for what it signals about San Francisco’s early approach: keep developmental pieces in the building while the larger decisions — bigger contracts, outside additions and potential departures — remain ahead. Zakelj saw only a small slice of action last season, but the organization decided he was worth keeping “for the time being,” per Yahoo Sports, suggesting the 49ers still view him as a viable interior-line option who can compete in camp and serve as protection against injuries.

For teams like San Francisco, the bottom half of the roster often determines whether a strong season withstands the attrition of an NFL schedule. Offensive line depth, in particular, is prized because one injury can force a reshuffle that affects the entire offense. Retaining players who know the system and terminology can reduce the learning curve, especially early in an offseason program when coaches are installing and re-installing concepts.

Zakelj’s limited workload last season also underscores how difficult it can be for reserve linemen to carve out roles on established units. Even so, the 49ers’ decision to keep him around gives them flexibility as free agency ramps up and roster spots are evaluated across the board. It also provides competition — a key offseason theme — as San Francisco looks to stabilize the supporting cast around its front-line starters.

The move comes at a time when teams typically prioritize internal evaluations before spending heavily on the open market. For the 49ers, keeping an in-house free agent is a low-drama, foundational step that can matter in the margins, especially for a team with postseason expectations and a roster that routinely faces high-stakes games where depth is tested.

Next for San Francisco will be whether this kind of incremental retention is paired with more aggressive additions or difficult decisions elsewhere.
