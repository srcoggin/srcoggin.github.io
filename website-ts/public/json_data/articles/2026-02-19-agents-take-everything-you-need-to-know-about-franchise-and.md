NFL free agency is nearing its annual spending spree, but the league’s franchise and transition tags are poised to keep some of the market’s most valuable players off the open market — or at least give their teams significant leverage before negotiations even begin. As CBS Sports noted in Joel Corry’s “Agent’s Take,” the tagging system is one of the NFL’s most consequential tools in the weeks leading into free agency, shaping which players can negotiate with other clubs and how aggressively teams can operate under the salary cap.

The basic significance is simple: a tag can prevent a player from reaching unrestricted free agency. In a league built around parity and roster churn, the tag is the mechanism that allows clubs to keep foundational talent in-house even when a long-term contract is not yet in place. It also can create a high-stakes standoff, with players seeking multi-year security while teams weigh cap flexibility and the risk of paying top-of-market money.

Corry’s breakdown explains the two primary options. The franchise tag is the stronger of the two, restricting a player’s ability to freely negotiate elsewhere. Depending on the type used, it can either fully block movement or allow a player to seek an offer from another team with the original club retaining matching rights and compensation protections. The transition tag, by contrast, is generally viewed as a softer approach: it gives the player the ability to negotiate with other teams while giving the original club the right to match an offer — without the same level of compensation safeguards associated with the franchise mechanism.

The nuances matter because the tag is not just a personnel tool; it is a financial statement. Tagged players are typically assigned a significant one-year tender, a figure that can consume a large portion of a team’s cap space and influence every other decision — from retaining role players to pursuing outside free agents. For players, the tag can mean a massive short-term payout, but it can also delay a long-term deal that protects against injury and stabilizes a career’s earnings.

For fans, the tag often answers the first major question of the offseason: Is a team prepared to risk losing a star? When a club uses a tag, it signals a commitment to keep negotiating — or, at minimum, to avoid watching a premium player walk away for nothing.

With free agency on the horizon, the league’s next wave of defining moves will come before the market even opens.
