Ravens offensive coordinator Declan Doyle is putting a spotlight on an annual NFL tug-of-war: the league’s voluntary offseason program and how much it really matters. With Baltimore beginning its spring work, Doyle made clear he hopes the team’s veteran players choose attendance, emphasizing that while organized team activities are not mandatory, they play a meaningful role in building a winning team, according to Pro Football Talk.

Doyle’s message speaks to a broader reality across the NFL. OTAs are voluntary by rule, and veterans around the league often weigh a familiar set of considerations — rest, family time, personal training plans and the wear-and-tear of another long season — before showing up in person. Coaches, meanwhile, regularly view the spring program as a valuable on-ramp to the season, particularly for installing offensive concepts, cleaning up communication and building timing that can be difficult to replicate away from the facility.

In Baltimore, Doyle framed the spring work as more than a check-the-box requirement. He underscored that the voluntary portion is “necessary to build winning teams,” a direct appeal for buy-in from players who have already logged years in the league and may not feel the same urgency as younger teammates fighting to make the roster. Even without pads and with contact restrictions in place, OTA practices provide a controlled environment for quarterbacks and skill players to develop rhythm, for linemen to align on protections and for an entire offense to speak the same language when the tempo increases later in the year.

That dynamic can be especially important for a team trying to sharpen its margins in a conference where small lapses can end a season. Baltimore’s offense, like any unit, depends on precise timing and trust — the kind that is typically accelerated when veterans and young players spend consistent time together on the field and in meeting rooms.

Doyle’s comments also highlight an ongoing cultural test for teams each spring: whether leaders show up, setting the tone for attendance and accountability. In a locker room built to contend, voluntary work can quickly become an unofficial expectation, even when no one is technically required to participate.

The Ravens will continue through the offseason calendar with additional on-field work later in the spring.
