The Las Vegas Raiders’ search to fill out their defensive staff is moving into a more defined phase, with the club set to interview Ben Bloom for its linebackers coach job, according to a report from Pro Football Talk. While position-coach hires rarely generate the spotlight of coordinator or head-coach moves, the decision carries real weight for a Raiders defense trying to sharpen its identity and consistency, particularly at a position group often tasked with handling both the run fits that keep a unit on schedule and the coverage responsibilities that determine whether big plays are prevented or allowed.

The report signals the Raiders are closing in on what Pro Football Talk described as “a key defensive assistant” hire. The linebackers coach role is central to the week-to-week functionality of a defense, bridging scheme and execution on the field. Linebackers are frequently responsible for getting the front seven aligned, communicating adjustments before the snap and reacting to the modern NFL’s constant stressors — motion, play-action and mismatches created by tight ends and running backs in the passing game.

Bloom’s interview also reflects the broader reality of how offseason rebuilding happens: not just through free agency and the draft, but through the daily instruction that turns a playbook into performance. For the Raiders, that means finding the right teacher and tactician for a group that must operate as the defense’s connective tissue — coordinating with the defensive line on gap integrity and with the secondary on coverage spacing and leverage.

Because the report focuses on the interview process, specific details about the timing of the meeting, other candidates or how soon the Raiders expect to make a decision were not included in the source material. Still, the fact that the team is moving forward with an interview suggests the Raiders are narrowing options and prioritizing the linebackers spot as a pivotal piece of the staff build.

For fans, the significance is straightforward: scheme changes and roster additions can only go so far without coherent development and weekly game-plan execution. The Raiders’ ability to improve defensively will hinge on details — tackling, angles, communication and situational awareness — that are often installed and reinforced by position coaches.

What to watch next is whether the Raiders turn the interview into a hire and how quickly the staff comes together around the defensive vision.
