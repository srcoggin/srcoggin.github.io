The Seattle Seahawks are headed for a major ownership transition, with the estate of late Microsoft co-founder Paul Allen beginning the formal process of selling the franchise just weeks after a Super Bowl victory, CBS Sports NFL reported. The move signals a significant shift for one of the league’s marquee organizations at a time when the Seahawks’ on-field profile is at its peak and the NFL’s business landscape continues to evolve around franchise stability and long-term leadership.

According to CBS Sports NFL, Allen’s estate has initiated steps toward a sale of the Seahawks. The report comes with another notable wrinkle: the estate is also in the process of selling the NBA’s Portland Trail Blazers, another cornerstone asset Allen controlled. Taken together, the dual sales underscore the scope of the transition underway and the degree to which Allen’s sports holdings are now entering a new era.

For Seahawks fans, the timing is hard to miss. A franchise coming off a Super Bowl championship typically projects continuity — from the front office to the coaching staff and the roster — yet ownership changes can introduce uncertainty even for successful teams. In the NFL, ownership is not a background detail; it can influence long-term investment, organizational philosophy and decisions that shape competitiveness over multiple seasons. The sale process will be watched closely across the league because it can affect everything from leadership structure to how aggressively a team positions itself in the years that follow a title run.

The Seahawks have been one of the league’s signature teams of the past decade-plus, turning Seattle into a consistent NFL destination and a national brand. Allen’s stewardship helped provide stability through that rise, and any handoff to a new controlling owner is a consequential moment — not only for Seattle but for the league, which places a premium on stable ownership groups capable of meeting the NFL’s financial and governance standards.

CBS Sports NFL’s report that the Trail Blazers are also being sold adds context to the decision, pointing to a broad restructuring of Allen’s sports portfolio rather than a Seahawks-only move. That matters because it suggests the ownership change is part of a larger estate-driven process, not a reaction to football operations.

What comes next will be the focus: how quickly the sale process moves, what type of ownership group emerges, and how the transition is handled while the Seahawks attempt to build on a Super Bowl-winning season.
