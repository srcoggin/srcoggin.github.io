Tyreek Hill’s sudden availability has injected rare star power into the NFL’s injury-and-roster churn, with the Miami Dolphins releasing the veteran wide receiver as he continues rehabbing a torn ACL suffered in Week 4 of the 2025 season, according to CBS Sports. The move creates an immediate ripple effect across a league that prizes explosive playmakers, especially among AFC contenders looking to upgrade their passing attacks without waiting for the draft.

Hill, who turns 32 on March 1, is “working his way back” from the ACL tear, per the CBS Sports report. That timeline — a major knee injury early in the season followed by a months-long rehabilitation process — frames the central question for any interested team: how quickly Hill can return to full speed, and how close he will be to the game-breaking form that has defined his career. For Miami, releasing Hill closes the door on one of the league’s most feared big-play threats and underscores how quickly roster-building priorities can shift when injuries hit and contracts come due.

CBS Sports identified several AFC contenders as potential fits, including the Kansas City Chiefs and Buffalo Bills. Kansas City is the most familiar connection: Hill previously starred there before his move to Miami, and the idea of a reunion will resonate given the Chiefs’ continued pursuit of championship-level offensive efficiency. Buffalo, meanwhile, has remained in the thick of the AFC race in recent seasons and would stand to benefit from a field-stretching receiver capable of changing defensive structures snap to snap.

The significance extends beyond the teams listed. Hill’s release places a proven, high-impact receiver into a market that rarely features players of his caliber without a trade cost attached. At the same time, the torn ACL adds a layer of uncertainty that will shape contract discussions and expectations. For contenders weighing short-term urgency against long-term risk, Hill represents both: a potential difference-maker and a medical evaluation that must be vetted thoroughly.

For Dolphins fans, the move signals a dramatic turning point. Miami is stepping away from a marquee offensive piece while he is still in recovery, a decision that will naturally raise questions about the team’s direction and how it plans to replace top-end speed and production.

What happens next will hinge on two tracks: Hill’s rehabilitation progress and the league’s appetite to invest in a 32-year-old receiver coming off a significant injury.
