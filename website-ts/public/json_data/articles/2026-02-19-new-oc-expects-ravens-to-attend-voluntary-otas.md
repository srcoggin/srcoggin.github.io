Declan Doyle is not treating the Baltimore Ravens’ upcoming voluntary OTAs like a soft launch. The team’s new offensive coordinator told ESPN that he expects all of Baltimore’s players to attend the voluntary sessions, framing the spring work as a chance to set a tone early and “start the next regime on the right foot.”

While OTAs are not mandatory under NFL rules, Doyle’s message carries weight in a league where participation often reflects buy-in as much as conditioning. For the Ravens, the significance is twofold: a new coordinator installing his approach and a locker room being asked to align quickly as the offense turns the page. The Ravens’ offseason program offers a first on-field look at how Doyle intends to shape the unit, from terminology and tempo to how responsibilities are communicated across position groups.

Doyle’s expectation also underscores how organizational transitions ripple beyond the coaching offices. Even for veteran players, voluntary sessions can accelerate chemistry and reduce the learning curve once training camp begins. Coaches frequently use OTAs to introduce core concepts — not full game plans — and to evaluate how players absorb changes before the summer grind. A full turnout would give Baltimore the maximum number of reps for the start of this install period and send a clear signal that the roster is aligned with the coaching direction.

The Ravens, long built around a physical identity and an offense tailored to their personnel, will now be asked to absorb a new coordinator’s voice and structure. Doyle’s comments indicate an urgency to establish standards early, an approach that can matter in a tightly packed AFC landscape where marginal improvements in execution and communication often swing divisional races.

For fans, the spring storyline is less about highlight plays and more about foundation. OTAs can reveal early indicators — who is in attendance, how quickly the offense is functioning, how seamlessly players are picking up the system — that hint at whether the transition will be smooth or require a longer runway.

The next checkpoints will be attendance and tempo as the Ravens begin voluntary work.
