The San Francisco 49ers moved early to keep a depth piece in place up front, signing offensive lineman Nick Zakelj to a one-year extension and preventing him from reaching the open market as an impending free agent. The deal offers the club continuity along the line at a time of year when roster churn can be sharp, particularly for teams looking to preserve as much of their core as possible while navigating the annual free-agency cycle.

Both Yahoo Sports NFL and Pro Football Talk reported the agreement, noting the central takeaway: the 49ers are keeping one of their impending free agents from hitting free agency. The extension ensures Zakelj remains under contract for the upcoming season, giving San Francisco another option in a position group where injuries and week-to-week lineup changes often force teams to lean heavily on versatile reserves.

Zakelj’s return matters because the 49ers’ offensive line is one of the franchise’s most scrutinized units, central to everything the team wants to do offensively. Even when a starter stays healthy, game-day depth can determine whether a team survives the inevitable attrition of a long season. Retaining players before free agency also can help a front office avoid overpaying in a market where offensive line depth is routinely in demand and where teams frequently prioritize experience and continuity.

The move also reflects a broader, familiar approach for San Francisco: handle select internal business ahead of the market where possible, then assess remaining needs once the league calendar accelerates. One-year extensions for depth players can provide flexibility for the front office while maintaining a baseline of stability in the locker room and meeting room, particularly in the trenches, where scheme and communication are critical.

For Zakelj, the one-year extension represents a chance to remain with the organization and continue competing for a role as the 49ers sort through their offensive line picture in the months ahead. For the 49ers, it is a low-drama piece of roster management that can pay off quickly if injuries strike or if the team needs options across multiple spots up front.

With Zakelj secured, attention now shifts to which other in-house decisions the 49ers prioritize before the market opens and how aggressively they choose to supplement the line.
