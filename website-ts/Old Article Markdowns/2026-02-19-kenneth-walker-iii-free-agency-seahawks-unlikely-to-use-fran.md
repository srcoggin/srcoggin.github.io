The Seahawks are not expected to use the franchise tag on Kenneth Walker III, a development that could send one of the most prominent names at his position to the open market and immediately reshape the league’s free agency landscape. CBS Sports NFL reported that Seattle is unlikely to tag Walker, a decision that would position him to headline a running back class in which he “figures to be the top running back on the free agency market.”

If Seattle follows through, Walker would reach free agency without the built-in leverage a franchise tag provides teams in negotiations. The tag typically gives clubs a mechanism to retain a player on a one-year deal while buying time for a longer-term agreement, or at minimum maintaining trade control. Passing on that option would signal a willingness by the Seahawks to let the market set Walker’s value — and invite rival clubs to bid for a player now being framed as the marquee running back available.

The potential availability of Walker matters because running backs rarely hit free agency with this kind of billing. Teams that view themselves as a backfield upgrade away from a playoff push, or those looking to stabilize an offense with a featured runner, would have a clear target. Walker’s status in the report as the top back on the market elevates him beyond the usual free agent churn at the position and into the tier of players who can influence offseason planning across multiple front offices.

For Seattle, the decision not to use the tag would underscore the broader roster and cap calculus every team faces in March. Even without specific figures included in the report, the franchise tag is widely understood as a significant cap commitment that can limit flexibility elsewhere. By avoiding that mechanism, the Seahawks would preserve options — whether that means reallocating resources, seeking alternatives in free agency, or addressing the position through other avenues.

The next steps will come quickly as free agency approaches.
