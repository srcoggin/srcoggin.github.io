The Seattle Seahawks are headed for a new era of ownership.

According to ESPN NFL, the estate of late owner Paul Allen announced Wednesday that it has begun the process of selling the franchise, a significant step that formally puts one of the NFL’s most visible and valuable teams on a path toward a change at the top. The move follows Allen’s wishes, as the estate said the process is now underway.

The development matters not only because ownership shapes a franchise’s long-term direction, but because the Seahawks have been one of the league’s defining organizations of the past two-plus decades, both on the field and in the marketplace. Any sale of an NFL team draws leaguewide attention, and Seattle’s place in a major sports market raises the stakes further for prospective buyers, the league office and fans wondering what, if anything, could change in the short term.

Allen, a co-founder of Microsoft, owned the Seahawks until his death in 2018. Since then, the team has operated under the umbrella of Allen’s estate, with the stated intention that his holdings would be handled according to his plans. Wednesday’s announcement signals the next phase of that process: transitioning the franchise from estate control to an individual or group that will assume full ownership.

While the estate’s statement marks the start of the process, it does not immediately clarify who potential bidders are, how long the sale could take, or what the final price might be. It also does not indicate what day-to-day impact the move will have on football operations in the near term. In the NFL, ownership transitions typically require approval by league owners, and the timeline can stretch across months as financing and league review play out.

For Seahawks fans, the news lands with a mix of uncertainty and curiosity. Ownership can influence everything from organizational philosophy and leadership structure to long-term investment in facilities and staffing. Even when front-office and coaching plans remain unchanged, the shift in ownership can reset expectations and priorities across the building.

What to watch next is the pace of the sale process and how publicly — or quietly — it unfolds.
