The NFL scouting combine is no longer the all-hands showcase it once was, a shift that continues to reshape how teams and prospects approach the most public portion of the pre-draft calendar. As Yahoo Sports noted in its coverage of this year’s event, “the days of the combine being a true showcase for all the best players in each draft is long over,” reflecting a reality in which more top prospects choose to limit participation — or skip key drills entirely — as they manage risk and aim to control their pre-draft narratives.

That evolution has changed the combine’s value proposition for clubs and fans alike. For decades, the week in Indianapolis served as a centralized proving ground, with the league’s best college players testing in the same environment and under the same timing standards. The spectacle helped drive debate, boosted prospects’ visibility and gave teams a baseline to compare players across positions. With participation uneven at the top of the class, however, the combine is less of a definitive scoreboard and more of one data point in a sprawling evaluation process that also includes college tape, all-star games, school pro days and private visits.

Yahoo Sports’ assessment framed the modern combine as an event with both “overhyped and undervalued” elements, even as it acknowledged that player participation has “taken a hit.” The overhyped portion, as the league increasingly recognizes, is the idea that one week of workouts can — or should — override years of game film. With high draft picks carrying enormous financial and competitive implications, prospects have become more selective about what they’ll do in a setting where a hamstring tweak in a sprint or a disappointing measurement can linger in the public conversation for months.

Yet the combine’s staying power comes from what remains important even when the biggest names are not all competing in every drill. Yahoo Sports pointed to “hidden elements” that still matter “to the league’s overall health,” a nod to the behind-the-scenes value teams derive from the event beyond 40-yard dash times and vertical jumps. The combine remains a rare opportunity for clubs to gather in one place, compare information in real time and advance the pre-draft process in a compressed window.

For NFL fans, the stakes are clear: the combine still shapes how teams set their boards and how the draft conversation evolves, even if it is no longer a complete, universal test for the entire class. With the league’s talent pipeline depending on sound evaluations, the event’s relevance now hinges less on viral workout moments and more on the parts the public rarely sees — and on how teams adapt as prospects continue to treat participation as optional.
