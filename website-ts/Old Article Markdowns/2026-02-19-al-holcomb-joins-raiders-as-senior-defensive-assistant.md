The Las Vegas Raiders continued to reshape their defensive staff by adding a veteran assistant with deep experience on that side of the ball. The Raiders hired Al Holcomb as a senior defensive assistant, according to a report from Mike Garafolo of NFL Media carried by Yahoo Sports NFL and Pro Football Talk.

Holcomb’s addition signals another move aimed at strengthening the infrastructure behind Las Vegas’ defense, a unit that has been a focal point for a franchise trying to find consistency. While the reporting did not specify Holcomb’s exact day-to-day responsibilities, the “senior defensive assistant” title typically indicates a role with broad involvement — from game-planning support to weekly opponent preparation and on-field coaching assistance — rather than a narrowly defined position group assignment.

The hire also underscores how teams increasingly build layered defensive staffs, leaning on experienced coaches to help implement scheme, improve communication and accelerate player development. For the Raiders, adding another voice in the room matters because defensive improvement often comes not just from talent acquisition, but from teaching, adjustments and the ability to match game plans to personnel from week to week.

Holcomb arrives with a reputation around the league as a long-time defensive coach, and the Raiders’ decision to bring him aboard adds experience to a staff seeking tangible results. In the modern NFL, where offenses operate at high tempo and coordinators are asked to solve different problems each week, senior assistants can play an important role in self-scouting, third-down and red-zone planning, and helping align coaching points across the roster.

Garafolo’s report, cited by both outlets, did not include contract terms or a timeline for when Holcomb will begin in the role. The Raiders also did not have publicly available comment in the source material.
