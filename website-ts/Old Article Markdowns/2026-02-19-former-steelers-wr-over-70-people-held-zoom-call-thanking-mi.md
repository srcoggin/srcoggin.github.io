A former Pittsburgh Steelers wide receiver is offering a revealing snapshot of Mike Tomlin’s reach inside the organization, describing a massive Zoom call in which “over 70 people” gathered simply to thank the longtime coach — a detail that underscores why Tomlin’s leadership continues to resonate across eras of Steelers football.

Nate Washington, who played wide receiver for Pittsburgh earlier in his NFL career, shared the account in remarks highlighted by Yahoo Sports NFL. Washington said the call included more than 70 people, all coming together with the same purpose: expressing appreciation for Tomlin. In an NFL defined by constant turnover — of rosters, staffs and sometimes even organizational identity — the image of dozens of former players and team figures voluntarily reconnecting to praise one coach speaks to the kind of culture Pittsburgh has long valued.

Washington’s comments also drew a connection between Tomlin and former Steelers coach Bill Cowher, comparing the two coaching styles. Cowher, who preceded Tomlin in Pittsburgh, has long been associated with a demanding, emotionally charged approach that helped define the franchise in the 1990s and early 2000s. Tomlin, in contrast, has built a reputation for steady messaging and consistent leadership, but Washington’s comparison indicates the former player sees common ground in how each coach relates to people and motivates a team — even if the styles differ in presentation.

That matters for the Steelers because coaching continuity remains one of the organization’s defining traits, and Tomlin has been at the center of it. The Steelers have historically preferred stability at the top, and player testimony like Washington’s provides a window into how that stability is maintained: relationships, credibility and trust that extend beyond the locker room and beyond a single season.

For fans, the significance is twofold.
