That pushes general managers toward a different kind of team-building approach — one that emphasizes identifying value, targeting specific scheme fits and winning the margins with second-tier starters rather than expecting a marquee name to solve multiple problems at once.

Even so, Prisco’s ranking makes clear the market is not devoid of impact. His assessment notes that while the 2026 class is thin at the very top, “some stars” are still projected to be available. That distinction matters for contenders looking to extend a championship window and for rebuilding teams seeking foundational pieces. In an environment without a crowded top shelf, the teams that do have cap flexibility can be more selective, and the stars who do reach free agency can command even more attention — and leverage — because alternatives are limited.

The significance for fans is twofold. First, a thinner market increases the likelihood of aggressive retain-and-extend strategies across the league, as teams try to avoid losing good players into a marketplace where replacing them may be difficult. Second, it places a premium on scouting and pro personnel departments: If the biggest names are scarce, the next best path to a better roster often comes from correctly identifying ascending talent before it becomes expensive, or from acquiring a player whose value rises in a new role.
