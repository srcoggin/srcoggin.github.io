The Seattle Seahawks, one of the NFL’s most stable franchises over the past decade, are heading into the offseason with a defining piece of business off the field: The team is being put up for sale. The Super Bowl champions announced in a statement that the estate of late owner Paul Allen will sell the franchise this offseason, a move that immediately adds uncertainty and intrigue to a winter usually dominated by roster decisions, coaching moves and free-agency strategy.

The news, first reported by Pro Football Talk, marks a significant turning point for a club whose modern identity has been tied closely to Allen’s stewardship. Allen, the Microsoft co-founder who purchased the Seahawks in 1997, became a central figure in keeping the team in the Pacific Northwest and building the organization into a consistent contender and one of the league’s marquee brands. The estate’s decision to sell now means the league’s ownership circles — and prospective buyers — will turn their attention to Seattle, a franchise with a massive regional footprint and one of the NFL’s loudest and most recognizable home-field environments.

The statement from the organization said the estate will sell the team “this offseason,” a notable timeline that places the process alongside key league calendar events. Ownership transitions in the NFL are typically complex, requiring approval from other owners and the league, and often stretching across months. A sale process can also reverberate across the football side, even when day-to-day operations remain unchanged, because it can shape long-term spending appetite, facility planning and the strategic direction of the franchise.

For fans, the news matters because ownership sits at the top of every major decision. Coaching hires, front-office stability, long-term player commitments and investments in facilities all ultimately trace back to the person or group writing the checks. While the Seahawks’ statement did not address how the sale would affect football operations, the timing ensures the topic will hover over an offseason in which teams typically define their competitive windows and allocate resources accordingly.

Allen’s legacy in Seattle looms large. The Seahawks became a fixture on the national stage during his tenure, culminating in a Super Bowl title and an era that helped cement the franchise’s modern identity. The move to sell, led by his estate, effectively begins the next chapter — and invites questions about who will own the team, how quickly a sale could be finalized, and whether the next ownership group will maintain the same organizational approach.

What comes next will be watched closely around the league.
