Brandon Aubrey’s rise from soccer convert to arguably Dallas’ most reliable scoring weapon has turned an otherwise routine offseason designation into a real roster pressure point. With the Cowboys kicker set to hit restricted free agency, CBS Sports highlighted the scenario that can make even elite specialists vulnerable: a team with cap space and a need at kicker can force Dallas to pay up or risk watching one of the league’s most dependable legs walk out the door.

Aubrey’s restricted free agent status means Dallas can retain him, but not without navigating the leverage that comes with the tender system. Under restricted free agency rules, the Cowboys can place a qualifying tender on Aubrey to gain the right of first refusal if another team signs him to an offer sheet. The concern, as CBS Sports framed it, is less about whether Aubrey wants out and more about whether another club could structure an offer that makes Dallas think twice — the classic “steal” attempt that turns a specialist into a front-office test.

The urgency is rooted in how quickly Aubrey became central to Dallas’ weekly scoring. For the Cowboys, the prospect of losing Aubrey carries extra weight because stability at kicker is hard to find and easy to take for granted. When a team has a long-range option who can consistently turn stalled drives into points, it changes how opponents defend, especially in tight games. It also reduces the variance that can derail a contender over the course of a season.

CBS Sports’ report centered on the idea that multiple teams could see Aubrey as a plug-and-play solution and attempt to pry him away via the restricted free agent process.
